01Supply, day to day

Where work waits and money leaks

Quotes can take too long while prices move under them, and dealer credit can grow. Invoices can drift from what was agreed.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Lists arrive as photos

    Dealers and builders may send photos of handwritten lists, or voice notes. Then someone has to type each line into 1C.

  2. 02

    Quotes go stale

    Cement and steel prices can move from month to month. A quote from last week can lose money today.

  3. 03

    Large, long dealer credit

    Dealers may buy on credit and pay when their own projects pay. Balances can grow quietly.

  4. 04

    Delivery days keep moving

    Sites can change delivery days at short notice. Calls about trucks then take up the sales line.

  5. 05

    Factory and import invoices

    Invoices can drift from the order, the delivery note or the agreed price. A missing e-qaimə loses input VAT.

  6. 06

    Acts of completed work

    Developers pay subcontractors on signed acts. Volumes billed twice, or above the contract, can slip through.

03Measured, not assumed

Where the money would come from

Building-materials suppliers would move the same buckets as a distributor. We would show a figure only once a sprint has measured one.

A sprint would measure this industry's value from a company's own data.

  • Extra sales

    Orders taken after hours and quotes sent the same day. Counted only against the sprint's baseline.

  • Cash in sooner

    Dealer balances paid sooner. Counted by the days saved.

  • Losses avoided

    Goods held back from dealers far over their limit. Counted as bad debt avoided, on the sprint's evidence.

  • Leaks recovered

    Supplier overcharges and double-billed acts, credited back. Counted only when the credit arrives.

  • Hours (shown, not charged)

    Lines no longer typed by hand. Shown, not charged.

04The Gate

What would always wait for a person

The sales manager, a key dealer's sales rep, the finance manager, the chief accountant or the logistics coordinator would approve. Nothing risky would move without them.

By rules you approve

  • Telling a dealer the list has arrived and is being priced
  • Delivery confirmations from the dispatch list
  • Reminders on approved templates

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Every quote, and dealers over their limit
  • Any price below the list
  • Payment proposals on acts, for a person to release in the bank
  • Supplier dispute emails
  • Every 1C posting

Never

  • Answering questions on drawings or specifications
  • Changing a contract or a credit limit
  • Signing an act or releasing a payment
  • Holding or moving money

06Short answers

Questions

Would it read drawings or review contracts?

No. Drawings, site reports and contract review are not in the plan. It would work on quotes, orders, balances and invoices.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
