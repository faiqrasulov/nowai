02Six gaps, every working day

Where work waits and money leaks

Work reaches a distributor through six doors: WhatsApp, phone, email, paper, the bank and state portals. Money can leak in the gaps between them.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Night orders can wait for morning

    Shops may send voice notes and photos of handwritten lists after the office closes. If someone retypes them into 1C next morning, some can get lost.

  2. 02

    Credit may be checked late, or not at all

    At night, nobody may be watching the shop's balance. Goods can then leave for shops already over their limit.

  3. 03

    Reminders may depend on who has time

    When hundreds of shops owe money, staff are likely to call the biggest and the loudest. Small balances can grow old quietly.

  4. 04

    Shops that paid can still get chased

    Bank transfers can arrive with vague payment notes. If nobody matches them to invoices, a shop that paid may still get a reminder.

  5. 05

    Supplier prices can drift from the contract

    Invoices arrive by email, on paper and as WhatsApp photos. A few qəpik more per jar, on thousands of jars, can be paid without a check.

  6. 06

    No e-qaimə, no ƏDV credit

    Input VAT needs a proper e-qaimə (reported). If nobody tracks which suppliers still owe one, the credit can be lost.

Trade and repair staff earned 749.6 AZN a month on average in 2025. So the case rests on money found, not on wages saved. verified

05The Gate

What would always wait for a person

Anything risky would wait at the Gate (the approval queue). A person would approve, edit or reject it, with the evidence in front of them. In the story, the finance manager, the chief accountant, the purchasing manager and the owner make these calls. In the day above, a sales rep, the payments accountant, the customer service lead and a key-account rep make them too.

By rules you approve

  • Orders within the shop's credit limit
  • Reminders from approved templates, each tied to one invoice
  • Credit notes below the amount you set
  • A reminder to a supplier about one missing e-qaimə

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Orders over a credit limit
  • Credit notes above the rule
  • Emails to suppliers about overcharges
  • Every 1C posting, in one batch for the chief accountant
  • Any message outside the approved templates

Never

  • Hold your ASAN İmza
  • Hold or move your money
  • Sign or issue an e-qaimə
  • Change a price or a credit limit by itself

06Measure first

What a sprint would measure, and what we still need to learn

A sprint would measure these on one week of your data. Some questions stay open until then.

What a sprint would measure here target

  • After-hours orders: how many, and their value
  • Overdue amounts, by age and by shop
  • Supplier invoices that differ from the contract price
  • Purchases still missing an e-qaimə

  • How much of the market still runs through small traditional shops unknown
  • How late shops really pay: no Azerbaijani data on late payment between firms was found unknown
  • Which WhatsApp opt-in and template rules apply to reminders unknown
  • Whether 1C import files would work for writing back, client by client unknown

07Before you ask

Questions

Would our shops need a new app?

No. Shops would keep writing on WhatsApp and calling as they do now. The replies would come under your company's name.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
