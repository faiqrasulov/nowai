01Admissions and fees, day to day

Where work waits and money leaks

Families can ask at any hour and expect an answer. Some fees may arrive late, and staff may answer the same questions every day.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Questions at all hours

    Parents may write about fees, places and visits late at night and at weekends. With nobody on duty, those messages wait until morning or go unanswered.

  2. 02

    Families go quiet

    A family asks, visits, then stops replying. A busy admissions office may not have time to follow up each one.

  3. 03

    Enrolment steps missed

    An admitted student can miss a document, a form or the first payment. A place is held, then lost.

  4. 04

    Instalments chased by hand

    Fees may be paid by term or by month, by bank transfer. When reminders are sent by hand, some go late.

  5. 05

    The same questions every day

    Timetables, uniforms, documents, transport. Staff may answer them one by one.

  6. 06

    Trial lessons with no-shows

    Visits, placement tests and trial lessons may be booked by phone. If a family does not come, the slot is lost.

04The Gate

What would always wait for a person

An admissions officer, the head of admissions, the bursar, a class teacher or the chief accountant would approve or answer. Families would always be able to reach a person.

By rules you approve

  • Answers from the published fee list and calendar
  • Timetable and document answers from school notices
  • Reminders on approved templates

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Every visit, trial lesson and placement test, until staff confirm
  • Fee changes, discounts and scholarships
  • Sensitive family cases
  • Every 1C posting

Never

  • Writing to a child directly
  • Tutoring, grading or advice on a child's progress
  • Mentioning courts or penalties in a reminder
  • Holding or moving money

06Short answers

Questions

Would it ever write to a child?

No. Messages would go only to parents, guardians or adult students. Children's data needs extra care, and a lawyer's written answer comes first.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
