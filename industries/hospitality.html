02Busy hours, thin margins

Where work waits and money leaks

Guests may call when the team is busiest. Food prices can change faster than anyone checks them. Both can cost money.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Calls missed at the busiest hour

    Bookings, banquets and catering enquiries can come by phone and WhatsApp at peak times. Some can go unanswered.

  2. 02

    Guests may write in many languages

    Besides Azerbaijani and Russian, guests may write in Turkish, English, Persian or Arabic. Front-desk staff can spend the day on the same questions.

  3. 03

    Paper invoices, drifting food prices

    Small suppliers may bring handwritten notes. A price rise on one line can go unnoticed for weeks.

  4. 04

    Corporate and event clients may pay late

    Conferences, weddings and agency groups may be billed after the event. Follow-up can depend on who remembers.

  5. 05

    Seasonal staff, contracts to register

    Peaks can need extra staff fast. A labour contract counts only once it is e-signed on EMAS (reported).

  6. 06

    Missing e-qaimə from suppliers

    Some VAT-registered suppliers may send no e-qaimə. Without it, the input VAT credit is lost (reported).

04Measured, not assumed

Where the money would come from

A sprint would measure this industry's value from a company's own data.

  • Leaks recovered

    Supplier lines billed above the agreed price, and input VAT lost to a missing e-qaimə.

  • Extra sales

    Booking and banquet requests answered instead of missed.

  • Cash in sooner

    Corporate, event and agency invoices paid sooner.

  • Hours (shown, not charged)

    Invoice typing and the same guest answers, done for staff.

  • Fewer fines (not charged)

    Seasonal contracts registered on EMAS on time.

These buckets would move. A sprint on one week of a group's data would show by how much.

3,500 AZN platform fee a month plus 20% of the value we measure (target).

05The Gate

What would always wait for a person

Anything risky would wait at the Gate (the approval queue). The purchasing, banquet, front-office, finance or night manager would approve, edit or reject it, with the evidence in front of them.

By rules you approve

  • Confirming receipt of a delivery note
  • Reminders from approved templates, each tied to one invoice
  • Answers taken from the hotel's own information

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Every booking and banquet confirmation
  • Compensation above the rule
  • Supplier dispute notes
  • Every posting to 1C

Never

  • Take card payments or hold guest money
  • Promise a room or a table staff have not confirmed
  • Change prices or menus
  • Hold your ASAN İmza

07Before you ask

Questions

What about guests who write in English or Turkish?

Azerbaijani and Russian come first (planned). Turkish, English, Persian and Arabic would come later, and would need a partner.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
