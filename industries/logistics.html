01Freight, day to day

Where work waits and money leaks

Much of the work is likely answering, chasing and retyping. Money can leak through late invoices and unchecked carrier bills.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Where is my cargo?

    Shippers call and write to ask where their cargo is. Without trip data at hand, a dispatcher may have to reach a driver first.

  2. 02

    Arrival times chased by phone

    A dispatcher may ring a driver more than once per trip for an arrival time. The answer then sits in a chat, not in a system.

  3. 03

    Quote requests retyped

    Requests can arrive by email, WhatsApp and Excel. If each is retyped before it is priced, a slow quote can lose the load.

  4. 04

    Late papers, late invoices

    Signed CMRs and delivery notes can come back days after unloading. Until they do, the invoice waits, and so does the cash.

  5. 05

    Carrier extras

    Subcontracted carriers can bill extras for waiting time, extra stops and fuel. If nobody checks them against the agreed rate, overcharges get paid.

  6. 06

    Several languages on one trip

    Drivers and carriers abroad may write in Azerbaijani, Russian, Turkish or Georgian. Messages can be missed or misread.

03Measured, not assumed

Where the money would come from

Most of the value here would be time: fewer status calls and less retyping. Time is shown in AZN, never charged.

A sprint would measure this industry's value from a company's own data.

  • Hours (shown, not charged)

    Status calls answered and requests not retyped. Counted as staff time in AZN, shown, not charged.

  • Cash in sooner

    Fewer days from unloading to invoice. Counted as cash that arrives sooner.

  • Leaks recovered

    Carrier extras without evidence, left out of the payment until the carrier corrects the invoice. Counted only when the correction or credit arrives.

  • Extra sales

    Loads won because a quote went out the same day. Counted only against the sprint's baseline.

  • Keeps customers (not charged)

    Shippers who stay because they always get an answer. Not charged.

04The Gate

What would always wait for a person

The senior dispatcher or a dispatcher, the sales manager, an accountant or the chief accountant would approve. Nothing risky would move without them.

By rules you approve

  • Status answers from the latest trip update
  • Driver updates logged to the trip
  • Invoice reminders on approved templates

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Delays above the agreed threshold
  • Every quote, and any rate exception
  • Disputes about carrier extras
  • Every 1C posting

Never

  • Filing customs declarations: a licensed broker files
  • Negotiating freight rates
  • Booking loads or promising truck capacity
  • Paying carriers or holding money

06Short answers

Questions

Would it file customs declarations?

No. It would read commercial invoices and packing lists and check them against past declarations. A licensed broker would prepare and file every declaration.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
