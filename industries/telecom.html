01Small teams, bursts of calls

Where work waits and money leaks

Mid-size providers carry the same kinds of work as large operators, with far fewer staff.

Drawn from desk research; a Discovery Sprint would test it on your own data. estimate

  1. 01

    Outage bursts

    One cut cable brings a burst of calls about the same fault at once. A small team cannot answer them all.

  2. 02

    Billing and payment calls

    Subscribers ask what they owe and whether a payment arrived. Each answer can mean opening more than one system.

  3. 03

    Technician visits moved

    Installations and repairs may be booked by phone and moved more than once. A crew can arrive to find nobody home.

  4. 04

    Payments from many places

    Money can arrive by cash terminal, app and bank transfer. While a payment is unmatched, a subscriber who paid can still be cut off.

  5. 05

    Cut-offs after late reminders

    If a reminder goes late or not at all, the line is cut, the subscriber calls angry, and some may leave.

  6. 06

    Dealer balances and e-qaimə

    Dealer shops order SIM cards, devices and vouchers. Where they buy on credit, which we have not confirmed, balances build up. Business customers need an e-qaimə for their invoices.

03Measured, not assumed

Where the money would come from

The money would sit in payments that arrive sooner and dealer orders taken on time. We would show a figure only once a sprint has measured one provider.

A sprint would measure this industry's value from a company's own data.

  • Cash in sooner

    Subscribers and dealers who pay before a cut-off. Counted by the days saved.

  • Extra sales

    New connections, upgrades and dealer orders taken after hours. Counted only against the sprint's baseline.

  • Leaks recovered

    Bandwidth and equipment overcharges, credited back. Counted only when the credit arrives.

  • Keeps customers (not charged)

    Subscribers who stay after an outage. Not charged.

  • Hours (shown, not charged)

    Calls answered and payments matched. Shown, not charged.

04The Gate

What would always wait for a person

A support lead, the billing manager, the sales manager, the field crew lead or the chief accountant would approve. Cutting a line would always be a person's decision.

By rules you approve

  • Known-fault answers from the provider's status note
  • Technician slots from the crew schedule
  • Reminders on approved templates
  • Small goodwill credits within the rule you set

Waits for a person, with the evidence

  • Every disconnection
  • Every tariff change
  • Compensation above the rule
  • Dealer orders over the limit
  • Every 1C posting

Never

  • Cutting off a subscriber on its own
  • SIM swaps, ownership or identity changes
  • Mentioning courts in a reminder
  • Holding or moving money

06Short answers

Questions

Why not the large operators?

They are not first targets. One large mobile operator launched its own Azerbaijani voice assistant in 2023 (reported). NowAI is designed for smaller firms, which have small teams and the same kinds of work.

Which languages would it use?

Azerbaijani and Russian, in text and voice, often mixed in one message (planned). Azerbaijani voice is the hardest part, so anything unclear would wait at the Gate for a person.

What if it gets something wrong?

Anything risky would wait at the Gate for a person. New rules would be replayed on past weeks first. Each role would start in review-only mode, with every action waiting for approval.

Where would our data go?

Only the data you choose, under a written agreement signed before the sprint. Where it is stored would be decided after a lawyer's written answer on data leaving Azerbaijan.
